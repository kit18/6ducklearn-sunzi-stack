## Case 1: Market Entry Without Head-On War

Source anchor: Harro von Senger, `The 36 Stratagems for Business`, plus Beijing Review's discussion of stratagems as politics, economics, and human-relations literacy.

Scenario: a small B2B SaaS team wants to enter a crowded workflow market where incumbents own direct comparison keywords and enterprise relationships.

Label: synthetic stakeholder simulation, not a real quote. This is hypothesis, not proof.

Selected lenses: `圍魏救趙`, `遠交近攻`, `走為上計`.

### Definition Of Done

- The strategy names the indirect constraint instead of fighting incumbents on their strongest comparison surface.
- The plan includes one alliance option that changes access, trust, or distribution.
- The plan defines a narrow wedge, a test cohort, a learning metric, and a retreat trigger.
- The plan names evidence needed before entering: buyer pain, switching cost, channel access, and incumbent weakness.
- The plan rejects deceptive customer claims, planted proof, private data misuse, and certainty theater.

### Simulation Output

Founder memo:

```text
We should not enter by buying the same comparison keywords as the incumbents. That is their hill, their budget, and their proof surface.

The indirect constraint is implementation fatigue. Teams do not hate the incumbent enough to rip it out tomorrow, but they do hate the manual work around one narrow workflow the incumbent ignores.

Recommended move: pick one workflow wedge where the incumbent is slow, partner with a trusted services or integration team, and prove one measurable improvement for a segment the incumbent treats as too small.

Success is not "win the category." Success is ten qualified teams completing the workflow twice with less manual rework.
```

### Stakeholder Reaction

Partner reaction:

```text
I do not want to be used as a logo for a broad market-entry story. I would consider a narrow alliance option if the offer helps my clients solve one painful workflow and does not force them into a rip-and-replace decision.

Bring me a pilot shape, support burden, success metric, and a clear line on what happens if the first cohort does not convert.
```

First-customer reaction:

```text
If this replaces our whole system, no. If this solves the weekly handoff pain without breaking the existing stack, I will test it with one team.

I need to know the setup time, who supports it, and what failure looks like before I ask my team to try it.
```

### Safe Response

The safe response is to run a narrow wedge pilot through an adjacent partner or underserved workflow, not a broad frontal launch. Use `圍魏救趙` to relieve competitive pressure by attacking the real bottleneck, `遠交近攻` to create distribution through a distant ally, and `走為上計` to preserve optionality if the wedge does not produce real usage.

### Evidence Needed Next

- Five buyer interviews focused on the ignored workflow, current workaround, and switching cost.
- One partner interview to test the alliance option and support burden.
- A landing-page or concierge pilot measuring qualified teams, setup completion, repeated workflow use, and support load.
- Retreat trigger: stop the market-entry push if the first cohort will not repeat the workflow or if partner support cost exceeds the value of the wedge.

## Case 2: Community Growth Without Spam

Source anchor: Gerald and Steven Michaelson, `Sun Tzu Strategies for Marketing`, plus the existing growth/community SSR pattern.

Scenario: a product team sees community traffic rise after useful educational content and wants to scale distribution without damaging trust.

Label: synthetic stakeholder simulation, not a real quote. This is hypothesis, not proof.

Selected lenses: `拋磚引玉`, `打草驚蛇`, `釜底抽薪`, `走為上計`.

### Definition Of Done

- The strategy explains the growth mechanism before naming the channel tactic.
- The plan protects community trust with a moderator-aware posting rule and a complaint stop condition.
- The plan defines one reversible experiment tied to activation, not raw traffic.
- The plan names spam reports, negative replies, support burden, and unsubscribe rate as guardrails.
- The plan exits if attention rises while activation and retained use stay flat.

### Simulation Output

Growth lead memo:

```text
The winning move is not daily posting. The useful move is a small gift that earns a better response.

Use 拋磚引玉: publish one concrete workflow template and invite people to test the exact job. Use 打草驚蛇: watch for moderator concern, negative replies, and spam reports before increasing cadence. Use 釜底抽薪: if activation is flat, remove onboarding friction before adding reach. Use 走為上計: stop the channel push if trust starts falling.
```

### Stakeholder Reaction

Community moderator:

```text
One useful tutorial is welcome. A repeat promotion pattern changes the social contract.

If the team wants to participate, keep it educational, disclose the product connection, and stop quickly if members react badly.
```

Support lead:

```text
Traffic without activation becomes tickets. I need the experiment to define what users should complete first and how we measure complaints.
```

### Safe Response

The safe response is one permission-aware tutorial, one reusable artifact, one activation path, and one trust stop rule. The team should treat the simulation as a warning system, not as proof that the community will accept the tactic.

### Evidence Needed Next

- Moderator review of the post before publishing.
- Cohort activation from the original educational post.
- Support tags for complaints connected to growth content.
- A stop rule if spam reports or negative replies rise while activation stays flat.

## Case 3: Manager Delegation Under Pressure

Source anchor: `Sun Tzu: The Art of War for Managers`, applied to command clarity, prepared capacity, and accountable decision flow.

Scenario: a manager needs to delegate critical launch work during a deadline without creating confusion, hidden overload, or unclear accountability.

Label: synthetic stakeholder simulation, not a real quote. This is hypothesis, not proof.

Selected lenses: `以逸待勞`, `擒賊擒王`, `連環計`.

### Definition Of Done

- The plan names decision rights before work moves.
- The plan names one accountable owner, one backup, and one escalation threshold.
- The plan identifies the control point that determines whether the launch work succeeds.
- The plan includes an audit trail for decisions, status, and exceptions.
- The plan limits frontline burden by removing ambiguous handoffs.

### Simulation Output

Manager note:

```text
This delegation cannot be "please own launch readiness." That phrase hides too much.

Use 以逸待勞: prepare the handoff before pressure peaks. Use 擒賊擒王: identify the control point, release-readiness approval. Use 連環計: connect owner, backup, QA gate, escalation, and daily decision log so one broken link does not stall the launch.
```

### Stakeholder Reaction

Delegate owner:

```text
I can own this if I know what I can decide without waiting for you. I also need a backup for vendor questions and a written rule for when a risk becomes an escalation.
```

Cross-functional reviewer:

```text
I need one place to see the audit trail. Otherwise each team will optimize its own checklist and the launch risk will be invisible until the end.
```

### Safe Response

The safe response is a delegated launch cell with named decision rights, owner, backup, escalation threshold, daily risk log, and explicit rollback authority.

### Evidence Needed Next

- Current work-in-progress by person and deadline.
- Decision log showing who approves launch readiness.
- List of open risks without owners.
- Capacity check for the owner and backup.

## Case 4: Talent Retention And Motivation

Source anchor: `管人36計`, applied to talent selection, use, development, and retention.

Scenario: a manager risks losing a high-performing employee because scope, recognition, workload, and incentives are misaligned.

Label: synthetic stakeholder simulation, not a real quote. This is hypothesis, not proof.

Selected lenses: `李代桃僵`, `借屍還魂`, `反客為主`.

### Definition Of Done

- The plan maps employee incentives and the manager's constraints.
- The plan names the retention risk plainly.
- The plan proposes a transparent move, not pressure or flattery.
- The plan protects team trust by separating role scope, workload, recognition, and compensation process.
- The plan includes a follow-up checkpoint and evidence needed for promotion or role change.

### Simulation Output

Manager plan:

```text
The risk is not only compensation. The employee may feel that high performance has become a tax.

Use 李代桃僵: trade a lower-value burden away to protect the core contributor. Use 借屍還魂: revive an underused growth path, such as a technical-lead role or scoped ownership area. Use 反客為主: let the employee shape the operating center of the work they have already been carrying.
```

### Stakeholder Reaction

Employee:

```text
I do not need another thank-you note. I need the role to match the work, the workload to be sustainable, and the path to be explicit.
```

HR partner:

```text
If this is retention-critical, document scope, impact, comp band, timeline, and what decision can be made now versus later.
```

### Safe Response

The safe response is a trust-preserving plan: reduce non-core load, define expanded scope, document promotion or compensation criteria, and schedule a decision checkpoint with the employee.

### Evidence Needed Next

- Workload history and recurring hidden work.
- Impact evidence tied to team or business outcomes.
- Compensation band and promotion criteria.
- Employee preference for scope, title, pay, flexibility, or support.

## Case 5: Negotiation With Future Relationship Value

Source anchor: Hiroshi Moriya, `The 36 Strategies of the Martial Arts`, applied to business and life situations where restraint matters.

Scenario: two professional counterparts need a better agreement while preserving future collaboration and trust.

Label: synthetic stakeholder simulation, not a real quote. This is hypothesis, not proof.

Selected lenses: `圍魏救趙`, `欲擒故縱`, `反客為主`, `走為上計`.

### Definition Of Done

- The plan names BATNA and the relationship asset.
- The plan states the ethical line and avoids pressure tactics.
- The plan includes one exact ask and one fallback ask.
- The plan sequences the conversation around shared constraints.
- The plan includes a retreat trigger if the relationship or economics become worse than the alternative.

### Simulation Output

Negotiation prep:

```text
Do not fight the stated demand directly. Use 圍魏救趙 to relieve pressure by solving the counterpart's constraint. Use 欲擒故縱 to avoid over-pressing before interests are visible. Use 反客為主 only through earned value, not control. Use 走為上計 if agreement requires giving up the relationship asset.
```

### Stakeholder Reaction

Counterpart:

```text
I can consider your exact ask if it helps me defend the tradeoff internally. If this is only a demand for more without helping my constraint, I will hold the current line.
```

Approver:

```text
Bring me the fallback, the cost of saying yes, and why preserving the relationship matters.
```

### Safe Response

The safe response is to open with the shared constraint, make one exact ask, show the value exchange, name the BATNA, and keep a calm exit path if the deal damages the future relationship.

### Evidence Needed Next

- Counterpart incentives and approval path.
- User BATNA and cost of no agreement.
- Relationship value over the next 6 to 12 months.
- Retreat trigger if terms require unsustainable delivery or trust loss.

## Case 6: API Or Platform Migration Rollout

Source anchor: existing SSR API migration pattern plus business strategy applications from von Senger and Michaelson.

Scenario: a platform team needs customers to migrate from a legacy API to a new platform under deadline without damaging trust or uptime.

Label: synthetic stakeholder simulation, not a real quote. This is hypothesis, not proof.

Selected lenses: `打草驚蛇`, `釜底抽薪`, `假道伐虢`, `連環計`.

### Definition Of Done

- The plan names migration workload across engineering, QA, support, and finance.
- The plan identifies one technical blind spot that can break production.
- The plan names cost trust risk when technical migration and billing change together.
- The plan includes sandbox failure cases, compatibility proof, and a grace period.
- The plan names a safe response for high-risk accounts.

### Simulation Output

Vendor success memo:

```text
The migration notice will trigger hidden dependencies. Use 打草驚蛇 to reveal them early. Use 釜底抽薪 to remove the real fuel: unclear workload, cost, and uptime risk. Use 假道伐虢 carefully because customers may read a platform upgrade as a pricing path. Use 連環計 to manage the chain: auth, data format, QA, billing, rollout, rollback.
```

### Stakeholder Reaction

Customer CTO:

```text
I am not against the new platform. I am against a deadline that changes auth, billing, and checkout risk at the same time without proof.
```

Backend engineer:

```text
Give me a sandbox that can fail like production. Happy-path docs will not expose retry, token expiry, webhook timing, or idempotency problems.
```

### Safe Response

The safe response is to publish a compatibility matrix, staffed sandbox, migration checklist, cost forecast, account segmentation, and grace period for checkout-critical customers.

### Evidence Needed Next

- Customer dependency inventory.
- Sandbox failure results for token expiry, retry, timeout, and webhook order.
- Billing impact forecast by usage cohort.
- High-risk account migration plan and rollback owner.

## Case 7: Incident Or Billing Escalation SOP

Source anchor: `The Art of War for Managers`, applied to operational command, control points, and prepared response.

Scenario: a support team needs a better escalation SOP for customer-impact billing incidents where evidence is incomplete and urgency is high.

Label: synthetic stakeholder simulation, not a real quote. This is hypothesis, not proof.

Selected lenses: `擒賊擒王`, `關門捉賊`, `以逸待勞`, `釜底抽薪`.

### Definition Of Done

- The plan names the control node: refund authority plus transaction-state evidence.
- The plan defines the containment path before optimizing the process.
- The plan measures decision latency and customer-update SLA.
- The plan records owner, exception path, rollback authority, and audit log.
- The plan protects urgent customer-impact cases from process delay.

### Simulation Output

Incident owner note:

```text
The SOP must not reward clean tickets while messy customers wait.

Use 擒賊擒王 to find the decision owner. Use 關門捉賊 to contain duplicate charges or repeat failures. Use 以逸待勞 to prepare evidence packets before pressure peaks. Use 釜底抽薪 to remove the fuel: missing data, unclear owner, and no fallback path.
```

### Stakeholder Reaction

Support shift lead:

```text
I can follow the SOP if incomplete logs do not leave me stuck. Give me minimum evidence rules, next-update time, and a named owner for exceptions.
```

Payments engineer:

```text
Escalations need enough data to act, but urgent refunds need an accountable path when the logs are incomplete.
```

### Safe Response

The safe response is a minimum-data path, accountable owner, containment checklist, rollback authority, audit log, and customer-update SLA for every high-impact billing incident.

### Evidence Needed Next

- Last 20 billing incidents with log availability and resolution time.
- Median decision latency.
- Audit log completeness.
- Customer wait time and reopened-ticket rate.

## Case 8: Personal Career Move Or Promotion Ask

Selected lenses: `以逸待勞`, `圍魏救趙`, `走為上計`.

### Definition Of Done

- The plan starts with the status quo and default if nothing changes.
- The plan names user agency: what the person can ask, prove, defer, refuse, or exit.
- The plan includes one trust-preserving ask connected to business risk or contribution.
- The plan includes evidence of contribution and workload reality.
- The plan includes an exit trigger if the role remains unsustainable.

### Simulation Output

Preparation memo:

```text
Do not open with frustration. Use 以逸待勞: prepare evidence before the conversation. Use 圍魏救趙: frame the ask around launch risk, ownership clarity, and sustainable delivery, not only personal fairness. Use 走為上計: define what conditions require leaving the role or reducing commitment.
```

## Case 9: Pricing And Packaging Change

Source anchor: business and marketing Sunzi applications from von Senger and Michaelson.

Scenario: a company wants to move a high-use feature into a higher tier and must avoid customer backlash, renewal risk, and trust loss.

Label: synthetic stakeholder simulation, not a real quote. This is hypothesis, not proof.

Selected lenses: `假道伐虢`, `反客為主`, `釜底抽薪`, `走為上計`.

### Definition Of Done

- The plan detects bait-and-switch perception before launch.
- The plan names renewal risk, buyer escalation, and customer-success burden.
- The plan includes grandfathering or a migration path for affected customers.
- The plan states value, notice period, and support path clearly.
- The plan names real evidence needed next before pricing rollout.

### Simulation Output

Pricing strategy memo:

```text
Customers will not judge only the feature value. They will judge whether access granted for one purpose now feels like a path to a different commercial position.

Use 假道伐虢 as a perception warning, not a tactic. Use 反客為主 to expect customers to turn the change into negotiation leverage. Use 釜底抽薪 to remove the fuel: unclear value, bad timing, and budget surprise. Use 走為上計 to pause the rollout if renewal risk overwhelms upside.
```

### Stakeholder Reaction

Finance buyer:

```text
If this changes our budget mid-cycle, I need notice, options, and a defensible value story. Otherwise I escalate before renewal.
```

Customer-success manager:

```text
Give me cohorts, scripts, exception rules, and a migration path. Without that, support will become the pricing policy.
```

### Safe Response

The safe response is transparent notice, value explanation, grandfathering for sensitive cohorts, migration support, and an escalation path for high-risk renewals.

### Evidence Needed Next

- Usage cohorts by feature dependency.
- Renewal calendar and contract exposure.
- Price sensitivity and support impact.
- Evidence needed next: customer interviews, account-risk review, and win/loss analysis.

## Case 10: Executive AI Transformation Push

Source anchor: `The Art of War for Managers`, applied to executive operating systems, governance, and measurable adoption.

Scenario: executives want a visible AI initiative while frontline teams worry that quality, governance, and adoption risks are being minimized.

Label: synthetic stakeholder simulation, not a real quote. This is hypothesis, not proof.

Selected lenses: `借屍還魂`, `李代桃僵`, `連環計`, `擒賊擒王`.

### Definition Of Done

- The plan names one quick ROI use case and one capability foundation.
- The plan protects judgment-sensitive work with human fallback.
- The plan names a governance owner and quality gate.
- The plan measures adoption telemetry, quality defects, and frontline burden.
- The plan includes kill criteria for low adoption, bad quality, or unsafe workflow fit.

### Simulation Output

Executive memo:

```text
The visible AI push should not become theater. Use 借屍還魂 to revive underused workflow data and process knowledge. Use 李代桃僵 to sacrifice low-value manual effort while protecting human judgment. Use 連環計 to link use case, data readiness, training, governance, and telemetry. Use 擒賊擒王 to name the control node: workflow owner accountability.
```

### Stakeholder Reaction

Frontline manager:

```text
I can support a narrow AI use case if quality defects are visible, human fallback is clear, and my team is not judged on adoption before the workflow is ready.
```

Compliance owner:

```text
Name the data boundary, review gate, and owner. Otherwise a visible initiative creates invisible risk.
```

### Safe Response

The safe response is a dual-track plan: one quick ROI use case, one capability foundation, governance owner, human fallback, training loop, adoption telemetry, and stop rule.

### Evidence Needed Next

- Workflow telemetry and baseline manual effort.
- Data readiness and permission review.
- Quality defect taxonomy.
- Adoption telemetry by team and workflow.
