# Career Negotiation Decision Review

## Scenario

A senior operator is carrying two roles after a teammate left. The manager says budget is tight, a launch is at risk, and the operator wants to ask for title, compensation, or workload relief without damaging trust.

## Verdict

Verdict: negotiate
Confidence: medium

## Decision

- Decision needed: whether to ask now for title/compensation change, workload relief, or a phased agreement.
- Deadline: before the launch plan locks for the next cycle.
- Default if no action: the operator keeps absorbing two roles with no explicit scope, reward, or staffing plan.
- Stakes: burnout, launch risk, trust with manager, compensation fairness, and future leverage.

## Mode And Terrain

| Area | Evidence | Interpretation | Missing / falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Status quo | Two roles are being carried informally | The real competitor is inertia, not the manager | Exact workload and outcomes delivered |
| Actors | Operator, manager, finance, launch team, missing teammate role | Manager has budget pressure and launch accountability | Who can approve comp or staffing |
| Incentives | Operator needs fairness; manager needs launch continuity | A direct demand may miss the manager's constraint | Manager's success metric this cycle |
| Constraints | Budget is tight and launch is near | Timing favors a phased ask with evidence | Whether budget truly cannot move |
| Timing | Launch plan will lock soon | Ask before scope becomes invisible | Launch milestone dates |
| Information asymmetry | Operator knows hidden work; manager may not see full load | Evidence packet matters | Calendar, tickets, incidents, outcomes |
| Trust / relationship value | Future cooperation matters | Use a trust-preserving ask, not a threat | Manager reaction history |
| User agency | Can document work, propose options, ask for decision date, set boundaries | Strong agency without coercion | BATNA if nothing changes |

## Selected Lenses

| Lens | What it reveals | Ethical translation | Falsifier / misuse risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 以逸待勞 | Preparation beats emotional confrontation | Build an evidence packet and choose the meeting timing | Falsified if delay worsens burnout or launch risk |
| 圍魏救趙 | Relieve pressure indirectly by addressing the manager's constraint | Frame the ask around launch risk, staffing, and sustainable ownership | Misuse risk: hiding the fairness ask entirely |
| 走為上計 | Exit or boundary-setting may be rational if the game stays unfair | Define a retreat trigger and alternative plan | Misuse risk: threatening before exploring options |

## Options

| Option | When it fits | Cost | Risk | Stop rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct move | Manager has approval power and values candor | Social discomfort | May sound like an ultimatum if unsupported | Stop if manager asks for data first |
| Indirect move | Budget is constrained but launch risk is visible | More preparation | Fairness issue may get diluted | Stop if no decision owner appears |
| Alliance/help move | Product or launch leads can confirm impact | Requires coordination | Looks political if framed poorly | Stop if allies cannot speak from evidence |
| Wait/prepare move | Evidence is too thin today | Burnout continues | Scope becomes normalized | Stop after one week or before plan lock |
| Retreat/exit move | No path to scope relief, comp review, or staffing | Search and transition cost | Relationship strain if abrupt | Trigger if no decision date or relief after agreed checkpoint |

## Recommendation

- Next move: negotiate with a prepared evidence packet and a phased ask.
- Exact ask or action: "I am carrying X and Y responsibilities. To protect the launch and make this sustainable, can we choose one of three paths: title/comp review now, temporary launch bonus plus decision date, or remove these responsibilities from my scope by date?"
- Leading signal: manager agrees to a decision owner, date, and explicit scope.
- Guardrail: keep the ask factual and trust-preserving; do not bluff with a fake offer.
- Retreat trigger: if there is no decision date, no scope relief, and no recognition after the agreed checkpoint, begin an exit or internal-transfer plan.
