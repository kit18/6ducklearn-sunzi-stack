# Focus Group Strategy Evaluation

This artifact is a synthetic panel simulation, not real quotes. It applies the PM/UED focus-group method to the top-10 real-world Sunzi and Thirty-Six Stratagems strategy cases.

## Source-Grounded Strategy Set

Sources used: `The 36 Stratagems for Business`, Beijing Review on Harro von Senger, `Sun Tzu Strategies for Marketing`, `The Art of War for Managers`, `Sun Tzu for Success`, `The 36 Strategies of the Martial Arts`, `管人36計`, and `職場孫子兵法`.

Panel roles used across the evaluation: PM lead, UED researcher, growth leader, operations/SOP leader, enterprise buyer, platform engineer, trust/safety reviewer, customer-support lead.

## Focus Group Method

1. Define the strategy and source anchor.
2. Define Definition Of Done before stakeholder reactions.
3. Simulate at least four stakeholder reactions.
4. Evaluate with scorecard, dissent, and risk.
5. Run Iteration 1 for the largest quality gap.
6. Run Iteration 2 for adoption, safety, or evidence gaps.
7. Mark final state as works after revision only when DoD is satisfied.

When user-intention scoring is needed, apply the User Intention Metric Contract: simulate Interview Responses and 360 Comments, draft a dynamic scoring contract, freeze the formula, extract structured signals, and calculate Deterministic Scores. `works after revision` means ready for real validation, not proven demand from synthetic NPS proxy, PMF proxy, willingness-to-pay proxy, or market proof.

## Case 1: Market Entry Without Head-On War

Source anchor: `The 36 Stratagems for Business`; Beijing Review on stratagem literacy for economics and human relations.

### Definition Of Done

- Identifies indirect constraint, alliance option, retreat trigger, and buyer evidence.
- Includes PM lead, UED researcher, enterprise buyer, and trust/safety reviewer reactions.
- Blocks deceptive claims and overconfident category-win framing.

### Stakeholder Simulation

- PM lead: "The wedge is promising, but the first version still sounds like market ambition before buyer pain."
- UED researcher: "A user can understand the narrow workflow only if the current workaround is named."
- Enterprise buyer: "I need proof this does not become a rip-and-replace proposal."
- Trust/safety reviewer: "The plan is acceptable if the proof is pilot evidence, not manufactured credibility."

### Evaluation Result

Verdict: revise. The focus group accepts the indirect-entry logic but rejects the first version until buyer pain, support burden, and retreat criteria are explicit.

### Iteration 1

Change: narrow the wedge to one ignored workflow and one partner-assisted pilot.
Result: PM and enterprise buyer agree the strategy is now testable.

### Iteration 2

Change: add support-load cap, pilot evidence, and retreat trigger.
Result: works after revision.

## Case 2: Community Growth Without Spam

Source anchor: `Sun Tzu Strategies for Marketing`.

### Definition Of Done

- Separates growth mechanism from channel tactic.
- Protects community trust and includes moderator review.
- Uses activation and retained use, not traffic alone.

### Stakeholder Simulation

- growth leader: "The mechanism is useful education, not posting frequency."
- UED researcher: "Users need one concrete artifact, not a campaign."
- customer-support lead: "If this raises low-intent tickets, support absorbs the cost."
- trust/safety reviewer: "The stop rule must trigger before community trust is harmed."

### Evaluation Result

Verdict: revise. The panel supports a reversible educational experiment but rejects automation until moderator feedback and trust guardrails exist.

### Iteration 1

Change: replace automation with one permission-aware tutorial and reusable template.
Result: growth leader and UED researcher approve the mechanism.

### Iteration 2

Change: add spam reports, negative replies, support complaints, and day-7 retained use as guardrails.
Result: works after revision.

## Case 6: API Or Platform Migration Rollout

Source anchor: business-strategy applications from von Senger and Michaelson.

### Definition Of Done

- Includes stakeholder loss/fear, migration workload, technical blind spot, cost trust risk, sandbox, and grace period.
- Separates platform value from pricing or lock-in perception.
- Requires rollback owner.

### Stakeholder Simulation

- platform engineer: "Happy-path docs will not reveal retry, token expiry, webhook order, or idempotency risk."
- enterprise buyer: "If cost and migration change together, I read it as commercial pressure."
- customer-support lead: "Support needs segmentation and exception rules before notices go out."
- trust/safety reviewer: "Do not hide cost impact inside platform language."

### Evaluation Result

Verdict: revise. The panel rejects the first rollout until technical and commercial risks are separated.

### Iteration 1

Change: add staffed sandbox, compatibility matrix, and failure simulations.
Result: platform engineer accepts the technical rehearsal.

### Iteration 2

Change: add cost forecast, grace period, high-risk account path, and rollback owner.
Result: works after revision.

## Cross-Case Evaluation

- Cases 1, 2, 5, 8, and 9 needed clearer trust-preserving asks before they worked.
- Cases 3, 6, 7, and 10 needed owner, fallback, audit, telemetry, or rollback clarity.
- Case 4 needed transparency around incentives and workload so retention did not become pressure.
- The strongest repeated dissent came from enterprise buyer, customer-support lead, and trust/safety reviewer.
- The focus group method improved the strategy set by forcing Definition Of Done before simulation and evidence needed next after simulation.
