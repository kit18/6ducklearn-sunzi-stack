# Macro Public-Sector Decision Memo

## Scenario

A regional government wants to attract advanced manufacturing investment, but investors cite slow permits, unclear workforce pipelines, and weak supplier depth.

## Decision

- Objective: increase credible advanced manufacturing investment without making unfunded subsidy promises.
- Arena/domain: macro and public sector.
- Time horizon: 18 months for pipeline conversion, five years for sector depth.
- Recommendation: create a focused investment vehicle that removes permitting friction, packages workforce commitments, and targets two subsectors where local suppliers already have an advantage without unsupported subsidy commitments.
- Confidence: medium, pending investor interviews and permit-cycle baseline.

## Strategy-Analyst Review

- Recommended governance vehicle: a focused investment vehicle with published authority, oversight, and permit-cycle SLA.
- Alternative lens: `遠交近攻` if distant anchor investors can change local supplier economics.
- Do nothing / retreat option: avoid broad subsidy expansion until pipeline evidence proves sector focus.
- Ethical rewrite: do not hide permitting, workforce, or community risks; disclose tradeoffs and governance.
- Kill criterion: stop the vehicle design if investor interviews show demand is too diffuse for two-sector focus.

## Action Plan

1. First reversible move: run 20 investor and supplier interviews to classify blockers and subsector fit.
2. Sequence: choose two subsectors, define vehicle authority, publish permit SLA, align training slots, and convert first qualified deals.
3. Owner: economic development lead with permitting, workforce, and community accountability board.
4. Leading metric: qualified investment pipeline with named blocker removed.
5. Stop condition: no signed investor commitments after two subsector-specific offer cycles.
