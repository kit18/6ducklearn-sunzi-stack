# Thirty-Six Stratagems Source-Story Reference

Use this reference to ground lens selection in the classical mechanism behind each stratagem, then translate the mechanism into ethical modern business strategy. The classical source is the public-domain `三十六計` text on Wikisource: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E4%B8%89%E5%8D%81%E5%85%AD%E8%A8%88

This file is a reference, not a permission slip. Use source stories to improve diagnosis, analogy quality, and review rigor. Do not use them to justify deception, coercion, illegal interference, self-harm, or exploitation of vulnerable parties.

| # | Stratagem | Classical anchor | Modern business invariant | Misuse risk to block |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 瞞天過海 | Routine visibility dulls attention, allowing a material move to pass under a familiar cover. | Material shifts can hide inside "normal process" unless observers inspect changed incentives, payload, and timing. | Hiding material facts from customers, investors, regulators, or partners. |
| 2 | 圍魏救趙 | Relieve pressure on one front by striking the opponent's exposed constraint elsewhere. | Do not only push against pressure directly; find the leverage point that forces pressure to release. | Irrelevant diversion that ignores the real customer or operational fire. |
| 3 | 借刀殺人 | Let another actor's existing motive or authority create the effect. | Align incentives so an already-moving force solves part of the problem. | Scapegoating, outsourced harm, or laundering responsibility through a third party. |
| 4 | 以逸待勞 | Preserve strength while the opponent spends theirs moving through friction. | Position, pacing, and readiness can beat frantic execution. | Passive waiting that misses a narrow market window. |
| 5 | 趁火打劫 | Disorder exposes assets, bottlenecks, and unguarded choices. | Crisis can reveal acquisition, hiring, or repositioning openings. | Predatory action against vulnerable customers, employees, or communities. |
| 6 | 聲東擊西 | Direct attention toward one place while leverage is applied elsewhere. | Separate attention surface from decisive leverage, especially in competitive response planning. | Deceptive signaling to customers, regulators, auditors, or employees. |
| 7 | 無中生有 | An initially empty appearance can become real after repeated belief and commitment. | Create low-cost options, prototypes, or narratives that test whether reality will organize around them. | Fake traction, fraudulent proof, or fabricated authority. |
| 8 | 暗渡陳倉 | A visible repair or routine action masks a quiet advance through another path. | Quiet channels can be better than contested obvious routes. | Bypassing consent, contracts, security boundaries, or governance. |
| 9 | 隔岸觀火 | Let conflict elsewhere reveal information before entering. | Waiting can be active intelligence gathering when rivals are exposing constraints. | Allowing preventable harm or contagion to spread. |
| 10 | 笑裏藏刀 | Friendly posture can hide adversarial intent. | Separate tone from incentives; trust patterns, not warmth alone. | Betrayal disguised as partnership. |
| 11 | 李代桃僵 | Sacrifice a less critical piece to preserve the core. | Trade a smaller asset, scope, or option to protect the system's vital objective. | Sacrificing people, trust, or long-term brand as if they were disposable. |
| 12 | 順手牽羊 | Take a small exposed gain while passing through. | Capture adjacent low-cost upside when it does not distract from the main objective. | Opportunistic overreach or taking what is not permitted. |
| 13 | 打草驚蛇 | Stir the grass to reveal the hidden snake. | Use probes to expose hidden risk, resistance, or decision-makers before committing. | Alerting a competitor or attacker without learning enough. |
| 14 | 借屍還魂 | Give a dormant body a new animating purpose. | Revive unused assets, channels, brands, or capabilities when the old container still has leverage. | Resurrecting obsolete systems without understanding why they failed. |
| 15 | 調虎離山 | Lure strength away from its favorable terrain. | Change the battlefield away from a rival's advantage. | Moving into a new arena that still favors the rival more than you. |
| 16 | 欲擒故縱 | Temporary release can create later capture. | Restraint, reversibility, or delayed pressure can reveal true dependence and timing. | Manipulative withholding or letting a controllable risk escape. |
| 17 | 拋磚引玉 | Offer a small thing to elicit something more valuable. | A useful artifact, question, or concession can pull out better information or commitment. | Baiting without fair value exchange. |
| 18 | 擒賊擒王 | Capture the controlling leader to collapse the system. | Identify the real control node, not merely the loudest symptom. | Mistaking a visible person for the actual source of constraint. |
| 19 | 釜底抽薪 | Remove the firewood rather than arguing with the boiling pot. | Remove the fuel source of a problem: incentives, dependency, budget, access, or demand. | Treating slow structural work as an excuse to avoid urgent containment. |
| 20 | 混水摸魚 | Muddy water makes fish easier to catch. | Disorder creates mispricing, talent movement, supplier openings, and narrative resets. | Unsafe execution, opportunistic confusion, or information abuse. |
| 21 | 金蟬脫殼 | The shell remains visible while the living body exits. | Preserve continuity for stakeholders while moving the core to a safer structure. | Misleading stakeholders about a material exit or transfer. |
| 22 | 關門捉賊 | Close the door before catching the thief. | Contain an issue before remediation so it cannot spread or flee. | Trapping the team with a larger risk, or escalating conflict without exit. |
| 23 | 遠交近攻 | Build distant alliances to improve leverage against nearby threats. | External partners can change local power balance. | Symbolic alliances that add complexity without operational leverage. |
| 24 | 假道伐虢 | Borrow a route, then use access to change control. | Distribution, API, marketplace, or partnership access can become strategic leverage. | Violating consent, platform rules, contracts, or trust boundaries. |
| 25 | 偷樑換柱 | Replace the load-bearing beam while the structure appears similar. | Swap the component, metric, supplier, or decision rule that truly carries the system. | Covert harmful changes or metric manipulation. |
| 26 | 指桑罵槐 | Criticize one object so the real audience understands the warning. | Indirect signaling can correct behavior when direct confrontation is too costly. | Passive aggression, ambiguity, or public shaming. |
| 27 | 假癡不癲 | Appear simple while remaining deliberate. | Underplay knowledge or ambition to preserve optionality and gather information. | Feigned incompetence that damages trust or accountability. |
| 28 | 上屋抽梯 | Lead someone upstairs, then remove the ladder. | Commitment mechanisms can force focus when escape routes create drift. | Coercion, entrapment, or removing legitimate fallback paths. |
| 29 | 樹上開花 | Make a tree appear to bloom through borrowed display. | Borrowed structure, partner credibility, or packaging can amplify a small asset. | Cosmetic signaling that hides weak substance. |
| 30 | 反客為主 | The guest gradually becomes the host. | An edge entrant can earn control by owning the workflow, data, or customer relationship. | Platform capture, lock-in, or role reversal without value or consent. |
| 31 | 美人計 | Attraction and desire weaken a stronger actor's judgment. | Incentives, status, beauty, convenience, or novelty can distract decision-makers. | Sexual, romantic, or identity-based manipulation. |
| 32 | 空城計 | Visible openness creates uncertainty when defenses are weak. | Credible ambiguity can buy time when direct strength is absent. | Bluffing about safety, solvency, compliance, or capability. |
| 33 | 反間計 | Turn the opponent's information channels against them. | Existing mistrust or intelligence channels can be redirected defensively. | Disinformation, impersonation, illegal interference, or social engineering. |
| 34 | 苦肉計 | A costly wound can make commitment believable. | Costly signals can prove seriousness when cheap words fail. | Self-harm, employee harm, performative loss, or manufactured victimhood. |
| 35 | 連環計 | Linked moves bind the opponent into a compounded disadvantage. | Sequence complementary actions so each move increases the next move's leverage. | Correlated fragility, hidden dependencies, or over-engineered schemes. |
| 36 | 走為上計 | When the situation is unwinnable, retreat preserves future options. | Exit, pause, or retreat can be the highest-quality strategic act. | Premature capitulation before testing realistic alternatives. |

## Review Prompt

After selecting any lens, ask:

1. Which classical mechanism is actually present in the business situation?
2. What evidence supports that mechanism, and what evidence would falsify it?
3. What ethical boundary would be crossed if this lens were applied literally?
4. What non-deceptive modern move preserves the useful strategic invariant?
5. What kill criterion tells us to stop using this lens?
