# Applied Use Case Map

Use this reference when deciding whether a Sunzi or Thirty-Six Stratagems task is really stakeholder simulation, a PRD/product-value review, growth review, SOP review, negotiation, personal decision, or defensive-analysis task.

## Research Pattern

Modern applied books and courses do not treat Sunzi or the Thirty-Six Stratagems as only military history. They commonly translate the material into:

- Business and management strategy.
- Negotiation and workplace communication.
- Daily-life and personal challenge framing.
- Sports, martial arts, politics, and competitive situations.
- Defensive literacy: recognizing stratagems used by others.

Source anchors:

- Harro von Senger, [`The 36 Stratagems for Business`](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_36_Stratagems_for_Business.html?id=WbPenQEACAAJ), frames the stratagems as adaptable management and economic-competition tools.
- Beijing Review's interview with Harro von Senger describes stratagem study as useful for understanding politics, economics, and human relations, including avoiding traps and observing situations: [part 1](https://www.bjreview.com/books/txt/2006-12/21/content_93832.htm), [part 2](https://www.bjreview.com/books/txt/2006-12/21/content_93832_2.htm).
- Hiroshi Moriya, [`The 36 Strategies of the Martial Arts`](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_36_Strategies_of_the_Martial_Arts.html?id=Sbck4AgFZzkC), is positioned around war, business, and life.
- Gerald and Steven Michaelson's [`Sun Tzu for Success`](https://sonshi.com/michaelson.html) applies `The Art of War` to personal goals and competitive situations.
- Chinese-language applied material commonly maps Sunzi and the Thirty-Six Stratagems to business negotiation, workplace, life, and interpersonal situations, for example [Cite Publishing's Thirty-Six Stratagems page](https://www.cite.com.tw/book?id=103463) and [Books.com.tw's applied life/workplace title](https://www.books.com.tw/products/E050166749).
- Public course material also positions Sunzi as decision, insight, and layout thinking for modern work and life, for example [this modern business and life strategy course page](https://www.eyeweb.com.tw/index.php?product_id=5115&route=product%2Fproduct).

## Root Use Cases

| Root use case | User asks | Best skill |
|---|---|---|
| Decision review | "What should I do?", "Should I act, wait, negotiate, or exit?" | `sunzi-decision-review` |
| Stakeholder stress reaction | "Simulate their stakeholder-style reaction", "What will engineering/customer/partner say internally?" | `sunzi-stakeholder-ssr` |
| Strategic diagnosis | "What is happening in this competitive or uncertain situation?" | `strategic-situation-analysis` |
| Defensive reading | "Is someone using tactics against me?", "What traps should I detect?" | `sunzi-decision-review` or `strategy-analyst-review` |
| Business strategy | "What should the company do?" | `sunzi-strategy-consultant` |
| Growth plan | "How should we grow or distribute this?" | `sunzi-growth-review` |
| Operations or SOP | "How should this process/runbook work under pressure?" | `sunzi-operations-sop-review` |
| Product value / PRD | "Is this worth building?", "Is this PRD ready?" | `sunzi-prd-review` |
| Postmortem / learning | "What can I learn from this conflict or failure?" | `sunzi-decision-review` |

## Daily-Life Translation

Use "daily life" only as a bounded decision lens, not as manipulation coaching.

Good daily-life use cases:

- Career negotiation, promotion, role change, workload boundary, or job search choice.
- Interpersonal conflict at work where trust and future cooperation matter.
- Resource allocation: time, money, energy, focus, opportunity cost.
- Crisis response: what to do first, what to delay, where to find leverage.
- Defensive literacy: detecting pressure tactics, traps, false urgency, or asymmetric information.
- Learning review: what the situation taught, what signal was missed, and what to change next time.

Avoid:

- Romantic or sexual manipulation.
- Coercion, public shaming, harassment, or intimidation.
- Fraud, false proof, fake scarcity, hidden terms, and impersonation.
- "Win at all costs" framing where trust, law, consent, or human dignity is treated as disposable.

## Mode Selection

| Mode | When to use | Output bias |
|---|---|---|
| Act | The user has agency and a reversible next move. | Small move, owner, timing, evidence, guardrail. |
| Defend / detect | The user may be facing pressure, deception, or asymmetric information. | Trap map, verification steps, safe boundaries, no escalation. |
| Negotiate | Both sides have incentives and future relationship value. | Preparation, BATNA, interests, sequencing, trust-preserving ask. |
| Wait / prepare | Timing is poor or evidence is missing. | Prepared capacity, missing signal, trigger to act. |
| Retreat / exit | Cost of engagement is too high or the game is unwinnable. | Exit path, loss cap, recovery plan, learning. |
| Observe / learn | User wants understanding more than action. | Pattern, missed signal, durable lesson, future detection cue. |

## Quality Bar

- Treat SSR as the cross-domain pressure test. Treat PRD/product-value review as one adapter.
- Start with the status quo. The real competitor is often the user's existing workaround or inertia.
- Name the cost of action and cost of inaction.
- Name the relationship or trust asset at risk.
- Include at least one retreat option for high-stakes situations.
- Use stratagems as analogy and diagnosis, not commands.
- Prefer the move that preserves future optionality unless evidence supports a stronger commitment.
